00 How to use this

You do not need to read it in order

The first three sections set up a way of thinking about what is happening. Everything after that is reference material: one surface per section, what it does, what it refuses to do, and the prompts to start with. Use the contents list to jump.

On accuracy

Every capability claim here traces to Shopify's own documentation, and each section ends with its sources. Community forums, agency blogs and news aggregators were not used for any capability, limit or plan claim.

On currency

This space moves quickly. Where something is early access or still rolling out, it says so. Before acting on a specific limit, availability or admin path, confirm it against current documentation.

01 Where we are

AI stopped being a feature you buy

It became a place your customers shop, and a way you run the store. Two shifts, happening at once, on different clocks.

The two shifts

Shift one  ·  demand side

Your catalog is already somewhere you did not put it.

Shoppers ask an assistant instead of typing into a search bar. Shopify syndicates eligible products to those assistants by default, and on several of them the purchase now completes inside the conversation.

Shift two  ·  supply side

The admin is no longer the only way in.

Sidekick sits inside Shopify. Official connectors put the same store behind Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot and others. Your team can run real parts of the business by asking for them.

02 A way to think about it

Treat it as one system, not a shortlist of tools

The useful question is not which AI tool to buy. It is which parts of this you control, which you influence, and which you can only adapt to.

Content slot  ·  systems thinking framework

Amanda's systems-thinking framework goes here, from the working document, with how it applies to AI in ecommerce operations.

The three rings below are a placeholder structure built from the research. Replace or reframe once the document lands.

Control, influence, adapt

You control

  • Product data and how complete it is
  • Your published policies
  • The store facts you publish to agents
  • Which channels and checkouts are on
  • Who on your team holds which access

You influence

  • Whether an agent finds you credible
  • Fulfillment and return reliability
  • How fresh your inventory and pricing stay
  • The quality of whatever you let AI draft

You adapt to

  • How each assistant ranks and words things
  • Which regions get in-chat checkout
  • What the models change next quarter
  • Attribution you can no longer see

Each AI channel controls the final ranking, wording and shopping experience. Being eligible does not guarantee placement.

04 Where buyers meet you Buyer-facing

Shopify Catalog and Agentic Storefronts

The part that is already happening whether or not anyone on your team has looked at it.

What it is

Shopify Catalog is a structured source of product information gathered from eligible products sold by stores on Shopify. Selected AI platforms, shopping sites and agents read from it. Agentic Storefronts is the admin channel that syndicates that data and controls checkout per channel, found under Sales channels, then Agentic.

On by default

Eligible stores are in Catalog and the channel is active without anyone turning it on. You cannot opt out of Catalog itself. You can block individual agentic storefronts, and opting out can take up to seven days to take effect.

What has to be true before you show up

Your store

  • Starter plan or higher, not in private mode
  • Not password-protected, B2B-only, locked or in development
  • Terms of service, privacy policy and a return and refund policy published under Settings, then Policies
  • Account in good standing: verified email, two-factor on, low chargebacks, invoices paid

Each product

  • A title, at least one image, and a price above zero
  • Published to Online Store, Hydrogen or Headless with an identifiable URL
  • Not in an excluded category such as alcohol, tobacco, weapons, adult, services, event tickets or live animals
  • Product-level opt-out is possible with Unlisted status or the seo.hidden metafield

Eligibility is reviewed over time, and eligibility does not guarantee placement.

The channels, and where checkout happens

This is the single most useful table in the guide. Whether the buyer completes inside the assistant or lands on your own checkout changes your margin, your data and your customer relationship.

UCP, and why a protocol matters to you

The Universal Commerce Protocol is an open standard covering catalog, cart, checkout, identity and the order lifecycle. Co-developers listed at ucp.dev include Google, Shopify, Etsy, Wayfair, Target, Walmart, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Salesforce and Stripe. It is Apache 2.0 licensed, and Shopify merchants are UCP-enabled by default.

A standard means you are not integrating with each assistant one at a time, and you are not betting your roadmap on one of them winning. It also means the rules you set once, on pricing, eligibility and shipping, travel with the product rather than living in a storefront the agent never visits.

What actually changes for your operation

Completeness is ranking

Titles, descriptions, images, categories, variants, prices and inventory. Data held in metafields has to be mapped, or it reads as missing.

Store facts are an asset

The Knowledge base under Sales channels, then Agentic, holds return windows, shipping and gift services. Shopify drafts facts from your pages, and nothing it drafted reaches agents until you publish it.

Some fields are inferred

Shopify infers parts of the Catalog record and labels them as discovery signals rather than your own text. Check the agent-facing view, not only your product page.

Legal disclosures should sit in the first 6,000 characters of a product description.

The fine print that bites

Several things simply do not work inside an in-chat checkout. If any of them carry your margin or your compliance, keep those products on referral, or switch direct checkout off for that channel and leave discovery on.

Not supported in direct checkout

  • Subscription-only products, bundles, products with customization or line-item properties, B2B-only products
  • Local delivery, pickup in store, pickup points
  • Any flow requiring the customer to sign in
  • Most checkout blocks: upsells, loyalty, consent, custom fields, trust badges. Meta supports none

Always comes back to you

All refunds for orders placed through AI channels must be processed through your Shopify admin. A published return and refund policy is mandatory to take part at all.

Your analytics will under-count this channel

In a direct checkout, Google Analytics and custom pixels will not fire. Only server-to-server pixels do. Expect gaps in GA4 and in ad-platform attribution for in-chat orders.

The admin still shows Sales, Orders, Sessions and Conversion per channel, and that figure combines referral and direct checkout. Performance analytics are not yet available for headless stores.

Do not let a dashboard that cannot see this channel decide it is not working.

Who owns the customer

Stays yours

  • You remain merchant and seller of record on every channel
  • Your customer database and full order history
  • Fulfillment, service and the returns relationship

Shared on direct-checkout channels

  • Order details including the customer's name, email, phone and address
  • Once shared, that data is governed by the AI company's terms, not Shopify's

Put it to work

01

Go and look. Open Sales channels, then Agentic. See which channels are on, whether direct checkout is on, and whether Shopify is managing it for you.

02

Audit the data. Find products missing an image, a category, a mapped metafield or a price. Those are the products no assistant will recommend.

03

Publish your facts. Review what Shopify drafted into the Knowledge base. Nothing reaches agents until you publish it, so an unpublished draft is a silent gap.

04

Decide per channel. For products that break in an in-chat checkout, choose referral deliberately rather than discovering it through a failed order.

05 Where buyers meet you Buyer-facing

Shop

Shopify's own consumer surface, and the payment rails now running inside other people's agents.

What buyers get in Shop

  • A conversational agent for search, recommendations and order help
  • Personalized ranking and storefronts
  • Shop Minis
  • A universal multi-brand cart, with multi-store checkout in the app

What outside agents get

  • Through shop.md and Shop's agent interface, outside assistants can search and check out using Shop Pay
  • Every purchase requires buyer approval unless the buyer opts into an agent budget

You cannot opt out, so operate it

Data and merchandising

The same product data that drives Catalog drives Shop. Posts and merchandising are yours to control.

Checkout posture

Direct checkout versus referral is a decision you make, product by product and channel by channel.

Spend and measurement

Shop Campaigns budget, customer-acquisition targets and segment overrides, with analytics under Sales channels, then Shop.

One conflict to verify for your markets. The Fall 2026 Edition describes wider availability for the agent in Shop, while the Help Center still scopes conversational search to the United States and Canada.

Sources Shop channel and Shop Campaigns documentation, help.shopify.com; Fall 2026 Edition, 28 October 2026. Accessed 1 November 2026.

06 Where you run the store Operator-facing

Sidekick

The assistant already inside your admin, included with your plan.

What it is

Sidekick is Shopify's AI commerce assistant, used to get guidance and complete complex, technical tasks in everyday language. It sits on most pages of the admin and in the Shopify mobile app, with nothing to install. Features and usage limits vary by plan, and it respects each staff member's existing permissions.

It asks first

Sidekick cannot change your live store without your approval. Work it drafts lands in a Store changes card that holds up to 50 changes, and those drafts expire 10 days after the last update.

The part people miss: Skills

A Skill is a saved, reusable instruction you trigger with a slash name, up to 50 per store and in some stores 25. This is the difference between one clever operator getting good answers and your whole team getting the same answer. Sidekick may also apply a matching skill automatically. Memory is on by default and keeps conversations for about three months.

/low-stock-alertMake sure I never run out of inventory
/shipping-auditAudit my shipping configurations

07 Where you run the store Operator-facing

Canvas

Designing the storefront by describing it. Announced 1 October 2026, in early access.

What it is

Canvas displays all your store's templates side by side on one board. You can ask Sidekick to build or change your theme from your description, or edit blocks, settings and text yourself. You reach it under Online Store, then Themes, by choosing New, or Duplicate in Canvas on a supported theme. Changes autosave, and your customers cannot view them until you publish.

The difference from the normal theme editor matters. There, Sidekick can only add sections and blocks your theme already includes. In Canvas it can build blocks the theme does not have, because it is writing real theme code and checking its own work.

The constraints that decide whether this is for you

Access

  • Early access, available only to certain stores
  • Desktop only
  • Your plan must include theme customization
  • Staff need the Edit code permission under Online store, then Themes
  • Shopify-developed and custom themes only. Third-party themes are not supported

Trade-offs once you commit a theme

  • A theme edited in Canvas opens only in Canvas, not the theme editor
  • Canvas themes do not receive theme updates, and their files cannot be downloaded
  • App blocks and app embeds cannot be added or configured in Canvas
  • No translation inside Canvas, and no per-market customization

Duplicate before you experiment. The original theme stays editable in the regular editor.

Where Canvas earns its place

Seasonal and campaign pages

A landing experience that would normally wait on an agency queue, built and reviewed in an afternoon, published only when you say so.

Testing a direction

Duplicate into Canvas, describe the direction, and look at a real rendered store rather than a static comp.

Second and third storefronts

Sub-brands and regional stores that never justified a full design project.

Not yet for a flagship theme that depends on app blocks, per-market customization or regular theme updates. Those limits are structural today, not a rollout gap.

Sources Canvas and its requirements and customizing pages, help.shopify.com; changelog.shopify.com and Introducing Canvas, 1 October 2026. Accessed 1 November 2026.

08 Where you run the store Operator-facing

AI connectors

Your store, behind the assistant your team already has open.

The surprise

This is a Shopify product, not something each AI vendor built. Shopify publishes official connectors for seven assistants: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Muse, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot. They all run on the same Shopify-hosted service, so what they can do is nearly identical. What differs is where you install it, whether results come back visually or as text, and the AI vendor's own plan and admin rules.

You install from inside the AI tool, not the Shopify App Store, then approve a data access level in your Shopify admin. One store at a time. Access is capped by the permissions of the staff account that connects, and you cannot lower a level you already approved without reinstalling.

Three sentences for your risk team

Nobody reviews the actions

Actions that connected AI tools take on your behalf are not reviewed or approved by Shopify.

You own the mistake

If an AI tool makes an incorrect change to your store, then you are responsible for that change.

The data leaves

After you provide access, that data is no longer processed by Shopify and is subject to the other company's terms and privacy policy.

The practical control is the staff account. The connector acts through one person's permissions, so connect with an account scoped to the job rather than the owner account. Revoke by uninstalling in the Shopify admin.

Prompts to start with

Morning checkShow me my 10 most recent orders.
PerformanceShow me my total sales for the last 30 days, broken down by product.
Acquisition@Shopify how did buyers find my store last month?
RetentionShow my top-spending customers.
MerchandisingMake a Holiday Gifts collection with my three bestsellers.
InventoryHow many Vanilla Bean Candles are left?
PromotionMake a 15 percent off code called COZY15.
ContentAdd my About page to my main menu and label it About us.

Where it beats the admin

A connector is not faster than the admin at admin work. It is better at questions that cross a boundary Shopify cannot see. Store performance next to the spreadsheet your planner keeps. Inventory next to the supplier email. A weekly summary that pulls from both and writes the update your team actually reads.

Keep the limits in view. It does not replace your Shopify admin, and it does not replace Sidekick. Large batches run one item at a time and may stop partway. There is no draft state and no version history, so approved changes go to the live store.

09 Where you run the store Operator-facing

Flow

The automation layer, and an honest account of how much AI is actually in it.

The AI is around Flow, not inside it

Inside a workflow

Flow's only native AI step is a Generate text connector, and it requires you to bring your own OpenAI API key. Shopify's own documentation notes it calls the legacy completions API.

There is no native summarize, classify or ask-AI action, and no AI conditions, as of 1 November 2026.

Around the workflow

  • Sidekick builds a workflow from a plain-language description. It is inactive by default and you must turn it on
  • Sidekick generates test events from real store data without changing the store
  • Sidekick can fix a workflow that will not save
  • External agents can manage Flow through the AI Toolkit, which requires a scope Shopify must approve

The pattern that works today

Describe the automation you want in words, let Sidekick build it, and read it before you activate it. The off-by-default rule is the useful part. A workflow that silently started running against your live orders is exactly the failure this design prevents.

Documented exampleI want to create a workflow that tags orders with fewer than 3 line items as "small".
Documented exampleHelp me make a workflow that sends a 'Welcome to the Gold Tier!' email when a customer's lifetime spend exceeds $1000.

10 Where you run the store Operator-facing, technical

Shopify AI Toolkit

For whoever writes code for you, in-house or agency.

What it is

The AI Toolkit connects AI coding tools to Shopify's docs, API schemas and CLI-based store management so they can help build more accurately. It works with Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, VS Code and others, installed as a plugin, as agent skills, or as a local server. Store actions run through the Shopify CLI's authenticated context, with the developer choosing when to execute them.

Why a non-technical leader should care: coding assistants guess at platform details and get them wrong. This is the difference between an assistant that invents a Shopify API and one that reads the real schema. It is a quality and rework question, not a new line item. It does not remove the need to review and test the result.

Three things to put to your development partner

01

Are you using the Shopify AI Toolkit? If their assistant is working from memory rather than the live schema, you are paying for the corrections.

02

What is authorized to touch our store? The CLI connector authorizes API access and nothing more. Know which store and which scopes.

03

Who reviews what it writes? Speed here moves the review burden, it does not remove it. Name the reviewer.

Sources AI Toolkit, shopify.dev; shopify.dev changelog, 9 April 2026; Shopify CLI connector, help.shopify.com. Accessed 1 November 2026.

11 What to do with this

Four decisions that belong to you

Everything else here is delegable. These four are not.

01

Your checkout posture

Which products sell inside an assistant, and which send the buyer to your own checkout. Driven by what breaks in an in-chat checkout, not by enthusiasm.

02

Who holds the keys

Connectors inherit one staff account's permissions. Decide which account, with which scope, and who may connect one at all.

03

Your data standard

Product data stopped being a merchandising chore and became distribution. Someone owns its completeness, with a number attached.

04

What you will measure

Given that client-side pixels do not fire in direct checkout, agree now what counts as the truth. Otherwise the channel gets killed by a dashboard that cannot see it.

Approval is the design, not the friction

Almost every surface here drafts and waits. Sidekick holds changes for review. Flow workflows start inactive. Connectors confirm before Admin API changes and before publishing a theme. The pattern is deliberate, and the organizations that get hurt will be the ones that treat the approval step as a formality to click through.

A person who understands the consequence approves the change, and that person is not the person who asked for it.
